## Safety first, plastic second

A cleanup is only a success if **everyone goes home safely**. Read these rules every time, even if you're experienced.

### Personal protective equipment (PPE)

- **Gloves**: thick reusable gloves preferred. Disposable nitrile is acceptable but generates more waste.
- **Closed shoes**: never sandals. Glass, metal, rusted nails are common.
- **Sunscreen + water + a hat**: heat exhaustion is a real risk, especially in subtropical climates.

### Dangerous items — DO NOT TOUCH

- Syringes / medical waste → mark location, photograph from a distance, alert a moderator/NGO.
- Drums or containers with unknown liquids → same rule.
- Fishing nets attached to wildlife → do **not** cut hooks/lines yourself; call a marine rescue NGO.
- Storm debris with sharp metal edges.

### Weather

- **Never** clean during storms, high tide warnings or red flag conditions.
- Check tide times. Many beaches become inaccessible at high tide.

### Group rules

- Buddy system: never alone in remote areas.
- A designated coordinator with emergency phone numbers.
- Brief everyone *before* starting on what to do if someone is hurt or sees medical waste.

### First aid

- A basic first aid kit on site is mandatory.
- Cuts: wash with clean water, antiseptic, cover with bandage.
- Any deep wound or puncture from rusted metal → seek medical help (tetanus risk).

### After the event

- Wash hands thoroughly.
- Check skin for cuts, ticks, or rashes.
- Hydrate.
