## Why sorting matters

If you collect 50 kg and dump it all in landfill, you've moved the problem but not solved it. If you **sort** it, a large fraction can be recycled, energy-recovered, or repurposed.

## Quick visual guide

| Type | Examples | Bin |
|------|----------|-----|
| PET (1) | Drink bottles | Plastic — recyclable |
| HDPE (2) | Milk jugs, detergent bottles | Plastic — recyclable |
| PVC (3) | Pipes, some packaging | Specialized only |
| LDPE (4) | Plastic bags, films | Plastic — sometimes recyclable |
| PP (5) | Yogurt cups, caps | Plastic — sometimes recyclable |
| PS (6) | Foam, packing peanuts | Usually landfill / special |
| Mixed/other (7) | Multi-layer | Usually landfill / energy |
| Glass | Bottles, jars | Glass — recyclable |
| Aluminum | Cans | Metal — recyclable |
| Iron / steel | Fishing gear, drums | Scrap metal |
| Fishing nets | Nylon, monofilament | Specialty recyclers |

## Don't break this rule

**No food waste, no biological waste, no hazardous chemicals** in any of the above bins. They contaminate the recycling stream.

## Where to take it

- Municipal recycling depot (most cities).
- Specialized programs (Net Free Coastlines, Bureo, local NGOs) for fishing gear.
- Industrial partner — TideGuard's roadmap includes partnerships with circular-economy companies who pay per kg of sorted ocean plastic.
