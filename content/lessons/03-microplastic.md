## What is microplastic, exactly?

Microplastic is any plastic particle smaller than **5 millimeters** — roughly the size of a sesame seed. There are two types:

- **Primary**: manufactured small (cosmetic microbeads, plastic pellets).
- **Secondary**: formed when larger plastic items break down.

## How it enters the food chain

1. Plankton mistake microplastic for food.
2. Small fish eat plankton.
3. Larger fish eat small fish.
4. Humans eat larger fish.

At each step, the amount of plastic in animal tissue increases — a process called **bioaccumulation**. Studies have detected microplastic in human blood, lungs and even placentas.

## The 'forever chemical' connection

Microplastic surfaces can absorb pesticides and other toxins from seawater. When fish ingest microplastic, they also ingest those concentrated toxins.

## Why citizen reports matter

You can't see microplastic from a satellite. But the model can learn the *origin* and *transport* of macroplastic from your reports — and that helps us forecast where microplastic concentrations will rise next.
