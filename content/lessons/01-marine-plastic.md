## Why is plastic in the ocean a problem?

Every minute, the equivalent of a garbage truck full of plastic enters the ocean. Most of that plastic doesn't biodegrade — it breaks into smaller and smaller pieces called **microplastic**, which are now found in fish, seabirds, salt and even rainwater.

There are three reasons marine plastic is a uniquely difficult problem:

1. **It moves.** Ocean currents and wind push debris hundreds of kilometers from where it entered the water. A bottle dropped near a river in Vietnam can end up on a beach in Taiwan.
2. **It accumulates.** Five vast "garbage patches" have formed where currents converge. The largest, in the North Pacific, is more than twice the size of Texas.
3. **It transforms.** Sunlight, salt and friction crush large items into microplastic that travels into the bodies of marine animals — and, eventually, into us.

## Where does it come from?

Roughly 80% of marine plastic comes from land: rivers, cities, fishing gear lost at sea. That means the problem is solvable *on land* — by reducing single-use plastic, organizing cleanups and reporting hotspots.

## What can a 14-year-old actually do?

A lot. In this course you'll learn how to:

- Identify and report marine debris in your area.
- Read forecast maps that predict where debris will accumulate.
- Organize and run a safe cleanup with your school or community.
- Sort what you collect so it gets recycled — not landfilled.

Your reports become training data for the TideGuard AI model. Your cleanups remove the actual plastic. Together, prediction + action shrink the problem.

### Key takeaway

> Marine plastic is a *transport* problem more than a *production* problem. Predict where it goes, intercept it, and you can dramatically reduce harm with limited resources.
