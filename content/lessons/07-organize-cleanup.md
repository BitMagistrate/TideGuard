## A simple 7-step playbook

1. **Pick the location**: use the TideGuard map. Choose a D+0–D+3 hotspot with safe access (no cliffs, walkable from a public path).
2. **Set a date**: weekend morning, before noon, low tide if possible.
3. **Coordinate with locals**: notify the property owner / local government / nearby NGO. In most countries you don't need a permit for a small beach cleanup, but courtesy matters.
4. **Recruit volunteers**: school class, scouts, sports clubs, social media. Aim for 10–30 people for your first event.
5. **Logistics**:
   - Gloves (1 pair / person)
   - Trash bags (2-3 / person)
   - First aid kit
   - Water + sunscreen
   - Scale to weigh collected mass (a luggage scale works!)
   - A simple banner / flag so people can find you
6. **Run the event**:
   - 10 min safety briefing
   - 90 min cleaning
   - 15 min weighing + photographing collected waste
   - 10 min thank-you + group photo
7. **Log it in TideGuard**:
   - Open the **/cleanups** form
   - Draw the polygon you covered on the map
   - Submit before/after photos
   - Enter total kg + number of participants

## Tips that make a big difference

- Print a one-page guide for volunteers (what to collect, what to avoid).
- Have a designated "media person" who takes photos for social media.
- Sort waste *during* the event into types (plastic / metal / glass / mixed).
- Recycle properly afterwards — TideGuard partners can help you find recycling points.

## Common pitfalls

- Bringing too few bags. You always need more than you think.
- Underestimating heat. Always plan for breaks.
- Forgetting to weigh. The kg metric is what unlocks XP and KPIs.
